# Reverse direction: evaluate uncertainty-aware estimators without using test cohorts for fitting

After seeing the Wood→Parse results, test a different question. Use *only* Parse's unshrunk public log2 fold change and its reported per-gene standard error as source. Predict the independent experimental response in Wood IL-2/IL-4, Kang IFN-beta, Waddell IFN-gamma (24 h and descriptive 12 h), and Cano-Gamez Th1/Th2 (cocktail, separate exploratory stratum). Reuse published lineage mapping, gene filters and donor/array contrasts from the prior benchmark; intersect observed genes across mapped source subtypes and target lineages for each comparison. When two Parse subtypes map to a single lineage, average their effects; for primary error propagation assume perfectly correlated estimation errors (arithmetic mean of SE); independent-error alternative is a sensitivity only. Estimates across lineages also reuse donors and PBS, so inverse-variance independence assumptions are approximate.

Compare own, equal half, equal all and predict-zero. Additional established statistical comparators: inverse-variance fixed-effect weighted mean; per-gene DerSimonian–Laird random-effects between-lineage variance tau²=max(0,(Q−(m−1))/C); use random-effects center μ=sum(x/(s²+tau²))/sum(1/(s²+tau²)); empirical shrinkage estimate τ²/(τ²+s_i²)×x_i + s_i²/(τ²+s_i²)×μ. Exclude nonfinite/nonpositive SE; do not impute unreported effects. These formulas do not themselves account for correlated subtype errors or guarantee calibration. No target-trained parameters. Evaluate magnitude RMSE, centered RMSE, gene ranking Spearman, type-max accuracy using fractional ties. Primary gate for a new cross-study recommendation: uncertainty-aware shrinkage must beat BOTH own and half on overall RMSE and type-max accuracy on Wood IL-2, Wood IL-4, Kang IFN-beta and Waddell 24 h IFN-gamma separately. Multiple stimuli/time points from a single cohort are not independent labs. Report failures, full compared gene count and target label support. No claims of inventing random-effects meta-analysis.
