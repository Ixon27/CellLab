# Stress test: 90 candidate cytokines instead of four

Added **after** seeing the four-way Xue confusion, so this is a new, deliberately harder benchmark, not a second preregistered success. Compare all ligands in the full public Parse/Allen uncensored release as candidates. Source = the same four human reference subtypes CD14, naive B, naive CD4 and CD56dim NK; retain only exact finite gene symbols for every ligand and every source subtype, then intersect each target's assay. Report counts. No training on the test study, no selecting the easiest cytokines. Own signature, other three and fixed half blend. Compute Spearman correlation across identical genes for each candidate. If tie, use expected top1, top5 and reciprocal rank under random tied order; report candidate name, rank and best wrong score. These are closed-catalogue ranks, not disease diagnoses; chance top1=1/N. Known stimulation labels remain hidden from scoring until all candidate scores are obtained.

Test all four eligible Wood cell types for IL-2/IL-4 (full six-donor means plus each individual); differentiated Xue macrophage means and individuals for the balanced four ligands; Yuan monocyte 24 h primary and 6 h secondary, where symbol-level effects derived by independently audited code. Context probes: Kang IFN-beta (four mapped broad types), Waddell IFN-gamma 24 h (four available broad types); no adding sources/contrasts after scoring to improve means. Equal lineage/ligand weighting within a study and explicit results for each ligand/study, not a pooled cell-level accuracy. Report wrong-guess ligand explicitly. For Xue IL-4 vs IL-13, separately estimate Spearman transfer of their average response and their signed difference, with a within-target reproducibility reference: correlate leave-one-donor-out Xue mean with the left-out donor for each. Use both complete assay overlap and the broad-catalogue universe. Shared-vs-difference is post-hoc explanatory; do not interpret a weak difference as proof of equivalence. Do not infer molecular mechanism from a correlation.
